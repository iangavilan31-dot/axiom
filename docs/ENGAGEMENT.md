# How AXIOM keeps you studying — the evidence, and what we built from it

Researched 2026-07 before building the SAT Math mode. Every mechanic below traces
to a real finding, not a vibe. Sources at the bottom.

## The five findings that shaped the build

1. **Retrieval practice is the single most effective study method.**
   Roediger & Karpicke: students who took *one* recall test kept ~80% after a week
   vs ~34% for rereading — a single act of retrieval more than doubled retention.
   Most students still reread (feels productive, isn't).
   → **AXIOM decision:** you light a skill by *solving real SAT problems*, never by
   reading. Reading is the reward for solving, not the task. The graph is a map of
   things you can *do*, not things you've *seen*.

2. **Spaced repetition beats cramming.**
   Cepeda et al. — 254 studies, 14,000+ observations — distributed practice
   consistently beat massing the same practice into one session.
   → **AXIOM decision:** missed skills drop into a **review queue** that resurfaces
   them; a skill isn't "locked" until you clear it again later.

3. **Streaks work through loss aversion.**
   Duolingo: users with a 7+ day streak retain at **2.4×** the rate of those who
   never start one; streaks lifted commitment ~60%. But guilt-based pressure is
   fragile — the durable version uses the streak as a *delivery system for progress*,
   not a whip.
   → **AXIOM decision:** a daily **streak flame**, but the payload is your growing
   score, not shame. Miss a day and you lose the flame, not your mastery.

4. **Visible progress + small wins + self-chosen goals beat external pressure.**
   Duolingo's shift from rewards to intrinsic motivation came from celebrating small
   wins, showing visual progress, and letting users pick their own goal (which
   increased engagement and ownership).
   → **AXIOM decision:** a **score-ring that fills toward 800** as skills light up,
   per-domain progress bars, and a **daily goal you set yourself**. The whole graph
   lighting up *is* the progress bar.

5. **Microlearning + variable reward + instant feedback drive completion.**
   5–10 minute chunks with completion markers; dopamine fires on recognition and
   unlocks; instant feedback with worked solutions. Gamified programs saw ~30–50%
   higher engagement/completion (Deloitte: +50% completion, +37% weekly returns).
   → **AXIOM decision:** one skill at a time (2–3 problems, ~5 min), **XP + a
   celebration burst** on every skill/domain clear, and an instant check with a full
   worked solution on every single problem — right or wrong.

## The through-line
The AXIOM graph was already retrieval-shaped: a node you either can or can't do.
The SAT mode makes that literal and honest — **when every skill on the SAT subgraph
is lit, you have personally solved a real problem of every type the test can ask.**
That's the "comfortable for 800" bar, earned by doing, not by reading.

## Interleaving (bonus)
Mixing problem types ("interleaving") is separately evidence-backed. A **Mixed
Module** pulls random problems across all four domains — the closest thing to test
conditions, and the antidote to "I only get it when I know which skill it is."
